# Phase R23 — Health, Safety and Environment

**Arc III · Executes after R18** · Status ⬜
Depends on: R12, R16, R18, R22 · Enables: R20, R24

---

## 0. Purpose

Turn safety from a random penalty into a discipline the player practises: a
barrier model with visible leading indicators, two separate safety dimensions,
and environmental performance that costs real money through real channels.

---

## 1. Goals

| # | Goal | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Incidents require multiple barriers to fail | Probability is the product of barrier failure probabilities (HS1) |
| G2 | **Every serious incident is preceded by detectable indicators** | HS3 — no serious incident occurs without prior visible warning |
| G3 | Process and personal safety are separate | A "cheap safety" strategy improves one and not the other (HS4) |
| G4 | Environmental consequence scales with sensitivity | The same spill volume differs materially by setting (HS6) |
| G5 | ESG affects the cost of capital | A poor record measurably raises borrowing cost (HS11) |
| G6 | Near misses are informative and free | Near-miss frequency correlates with subsequent serious-incident probability (HS14) |

## 2. Design decisions

### 2.1 The bow-tie is the model

Threats → preventive barriers → top event → mitigating barriers → consequences
([14_HSE](../design/14_HSE.md) §2).

*Rationale:* it is how the industry actually reasons, it gives the player things
to *buy and maintain* rather than a probability to endure, and it produces a fat
tail the player controls — which is what makes safety investment feel like the
insurance it is.

### 2.2 Barrier condition **is** equipment condition

No parallel "safety stat". A barrier's strength is the state of the physical
equipment, plus competency and procedural compliance — all of which already
exist from R12 and R18.

*Rationale:* a separate safety number would be a second representation of one
fact, which architectural law L5 forbids. It would also drift from the plant.

### 2.3 Two safety metrics, tracked and displayed separately

Personal safety is common, low-severity and **cheap** to improve. Process safety
is rare, catastrophic and **expensive**.

**Design intent, stated so it is testable:** a player should be able to make
their injury rate look excellent while process-safety barriers degrade — and the
leading indicators should say so, loudly, to anyone who looks. HS3 and HS4 are
what hold this honest.

### 2.4 Near misses are generated by the same model

A threat that passes some barriers but not all is a near miss. It costs almost
nothing and it points exactly at which barriers are weakening.

*Rationale:* it converts the barrier model from a hidden calculation into a
readable signal, at zero extra modelling cost.

### 2.5 Environmental consequence is multiplied by sensitivity

From [R22](R22_ENVIRONMENT.md)'s setting. **This is what makes sensitivity a real
factor in acreage selection**, rather than a label on a map.

### 2.6 ESG closes a capital loop

Emissions intensity, methane intensity, flaring intensity, safety record and
spill record aggregate into a standing that changes the borrowing rate
([17_CROSS_IMPACT_MATRIX](../design/17_CROSS_IMPACT_MATRIX.md) §3.8).

**A slow reinforcing loop with two exits** — reduce emissions, or improve the
safety record — per design rule CI4.

### 2.7 Fatalities are modelled, soberly

Per open decision HS-D1. Treating them seriously is more respectful of the
subject than eliding it, and it is what makes process safety weigh correctly
against its cost. **Never scored, never gamified** — reported factually, with
regulatory and human consequence.

---

### 2.8 Physical effects at stage 4; administrative consequences at stage 9

Hazard draws, top events and the resulting equipment unavailability happen at
tick stage 4, **before the flow solve**, so an incident's production impact lands
in the same tick. Penalties, investigations, barrier updates, social licence and
ESG are computed at stage 9, so their effects land the *next* tick.

**The plant reacts immediately; the paperwork follows.** See
[21_INTEGRATION](../design/21_INTEGRATION.md) §4.1(a).

### 2.9 The ESG loop is the slowest coupling in the game

At 3–10 years it is class **P5**, which under integration rule IR1 obliges a P2
leading indicator: **ESG standing is published every tick** and its effect on the
borrowing rate is always visible. Under rule IR2 it also needs a standing
indicator visible without being sought, and under I3 an entry event —
`hse.emissionsThreshold` — firing while at least two exits remain.

Registration of all three is checked structurally by I-V1, I-V2 and I-V10.

## 4. Verification

The HS1–HS14 suite from [14_HSE](../design/14_HSE.md) §10, plus:

| # | Test | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| R23-V15 | No parallel safety state | Architecture test: barrier strength derives from equipment condition and is not separately stored |
| R23-V16 | Loop exits | The ESG loop has at least two demonstrated escape routes (CI4) |
| R23-V17 | Findings gate restart | Operations suspended by an order cannot resume until findings are closed |
| R23-V18 | Fatigue coupling | R12 crew configuration measurably changes the human-error threat rate |
| R23-V19 | Emissions to economics | Carbon price flows to the cost ledger; methane leaks appear as both lost revenue and penalty |
| R23-V20 | Stage split | An incident removes equipment from the same tick's solve; its penalty and investigation land the next tick |
| R23-V21 | Slow-loop registration (I-V1, I-V2, I-V10) | The ESG loop has a P2 indicator, a standing indicator and an entry event — all registered |
| R23-V22 | Consequence attribution (I-V12) | `finance.borrowingBaseChanged` from an ESG cause names the entry event and the tick it fired on |

**HS3 is the phase's gate.** If a serious incident can occur with no prior
detectable indicator, the model has become random punishment and the phase has
failed regardless of what else passes.

## 5. Out of scope

Detailed reliability engineering (R18 owns condition and failure rates).
Personnel-level safety-case modelling. Climate-scenario modelling beyond a carbon
price trajectory.

---

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| HSE reads as a morality system | It is modelled purely as cost, liability and access. The incentive to operate well is economic |
| The barrier model is opaque to players | Barrier status is a first-class read-model projection with overdue work listed; `hse.barrierDegraded` is a prominent event |
| Fatality modelling is handled badly | Sober presentation, never scored, never celebrated. Reviewed explicitly before shipping |
| ESG effects are too slow to notice | The standing and its rate effect are always visible, and `hse.socialLicence` fires on threshold crossings |
| Incident tuning makes the game punishing | Hazard intensity is a modifier ([18](../design/18_GAME_MODES.md) §5), including off, and SC1 band-tests the incident rate |
